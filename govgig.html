Problem

When I started on GovGig I asked, How do current day-to-day operations look like?

Stakeholder workshop sessions revealed insights into how contractors use software in practice. I found that…

  1. Customization was necessary to enable companies to tailor the platform to their workflows.
  2. Familiar UX/UI patterns were needed to bridge existing workflows and real-life tools.
  3. Evolving industry needs meant we needed scalable and functional solutions.

Action

I kept contractors in the loop from the first workshop to the last handoff, so every design decision was grounded in how they actually work.

From findings to features

Each finding became a design decision, and each decision shipped as a feature.

01

Finding

Customization was necessary

What I did

Instead of one fixed home screen, I designed the dashboard as modular widgets each company can arrange around its own work, and gave every company a profile it can shape for the contracts it wants to win.

02

Finding

Familiar UX/UI patterns were needed

What I did

I modelled key screens on tools crews already trust. The schedule reads like the Gantt charts and spreadsheets teams use on site, and switching projects works like a familiar search-and-filter menu.

03

Finding

Scalable, functional solutions were needed

What I did

I built a shared design system of [X] components and patterns, so new modules like the Submittal Register below could be designed and shipped without starting from scratch.

Deep dive

Submittal Register: catching risks before they slip

Submittals were tracked in [spreadsheets and email threads], so a missed government approval window often only surfaced once it had already delayed the schedule.

I designed a single register that puts what needs attention first: monthly KPIs, how long items sit at each stage, critical-path risks, the 14-day government approval windows, and how each subcontractor is performing.

Designing with AI

AI that fits into the workflow, not on top of it

Contractors don't have time to learn a new way of working, so I placed AI where the work already happens and kept a person in charge of every decision.

Ask GovGig

Always one click away in the top bar, answering questions using the project's own documents.

Playbook agents

Run specification checks section by section and sort findings by priority, so reviewers start with what matters most.

A human in the loop

AI output is its own stage in the submittal pipeline, so a specialist always reviews it before anything goes to the government.
